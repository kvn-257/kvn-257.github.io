TL;DR

The most central node in a network carries the most shortest-path traffic, which makes it the network's bottleneck. Adding one well-chosen link can relieve it. We consider the problem of finding the missing link whose addition minimizes the network's maximum betweenness.

Brute force recomputes betweenness for every candidate link, which is too slow even for moderate networks. We instead train a Graph Neural Network that ranks missing links by how much they would reduce maximum betweenness, using a joint classification and triplet ranking loss.

On scale-free Barabási–Albert networks, the model reaches 0.82 mAP@10 and 0.92 mAP@100, about as good as on high-diameter path and tree networks, where useful links are much easier to find.

Why Minimize Maximum Betweenness?

Betweenness centrality measures how often a node lies on the shortest paths between other pairs of nodes, so it estimates how much traffic flows through that node. The highest value in a network, its maximum betweenness, marks the most loaded node. That node needs the most maintenance and resources, causes congestion, concentrates power, and makes the network vulnerable to attack and fragmentation.

One way to reduce this load is to divert traffic through a new link. Given a graph \(G = (V, E)\) and its set of missing links \(\bar{E}\), we want the link whose addition gives the lowest maximum betweenness:

$$e_{\min} = \arg\min_{e \in \bar{E}} \; \max_{v \in V} \; BC_{G_e}(v), \qquad G_e = (V, E \cup \{e\})$$

Brute force tries all \(K = \binom{|V|}{2} - |E|\) candidate links and recomputes betweenness with Brandes' algorithm each time. That costs \(O(K \cdot |V||E|)\), or \(O(|V|^3|E|)\) on sparse real-world networks, which is too slow even for moderate-size networks. This motivates a learned approach.

Core Contributions

A New Link Prediction Task

We study link prediction that lowers the maximum betweenness of a network. Prior work mostly increases one node's centrality instead.

Classify-then-Rank GNN

A GraphSAGE encoder feeds two MLPs: one decides whether a missing edge helps, the other ranks edges by how much they help.

Triplet Margin Ranking Loss

A ranking loss that compares three edges at a time. It outperforms the standard pairwise margin loss.

How It Works

1. Learn node embeddings with a GNN.
A GraphSAGE encoder with a mean aggregator builds node representations through message passing. Because it is inductive, it generalizes to unseen nodes and graphs. A missing edge \((u, v)\) is represented by concatenating its endpoints' embeddings, \(E_{u,v} = [\,h_u \,\|\, h_v\,]\).

2. Classify and score every missing edge.
MLP1 predicts whether the edge can lower maximum betweenness at all (binary cross-entropy loss \(\mathcal{L}_C\)). MLP2 outputs a score used to rank all missing edges.

3. Train with a joint classification and ranking objective.
The ranking loss compares triplets of edges \(\{m, n, o\}\). The total loss \(\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_C + \mathcal{L}_R\) is backpropagated through both MLPs and the GNN.

$$\mathcal{L}_R = \max\!\big(0,\; -I_{m,n}(s_m - s_n) + M,\; -I_{n,o}(s_n - s_o) + M,\; -I_{o,m}(s_o - s_m) + M\big)$$

\(s\) are predicted scores, \(I_{m,n} = 1\) if edge \(m\) truly reduces maximum betweenness more than \(n\) (else \(-1\)), and the margin \(M = 1\).

Most Edges Don't Help: Sampling the Extremes

We train on synthetic Barabási–Albert (scale-free), Erdős–Rényi (random), tree and path networks with 160–250 nodes. For each network we add every missing edge, one at a time, and record the exact change in maximum betweenness as ground truth.

Almost all edges change maximum betweenness by roughly zero. Only a few at either end of the sorted list matter. We therefore sample training edges with a \(y = x^4\) distribution, which draws most samples from those two ends.

Accurate Rankings on Real-World-Like Networks

We compare the predicted edge ranking with the ground-truth ranking using mAP@k and Pearson (PC), Spearman (SC) and Kendall (KC) correlation over the top-k edges.

BA networks have a scale-free degree distribution dominated by a few hubs, like real social and transport networks. On them the model reaches 0.82 mAP@10 and 0.92 mAP@100, about as good as on path and tree networks, where adding an edge between distant nodes cuts maximum betweenness sharply and good links are easier to find.
