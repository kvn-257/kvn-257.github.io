Don't Get Stuck: A Metric for Scanpath Prediction

A scanpath model looks at a video and predicts, frame by frame, where a person would look. How do we score such a prediction?

The simplest idea is to check, at every frame, how salient the predicted point is (how many people actually looked there) and add it up. But this rewards a model that finds the most salient object and parks its gaze on it for the whole video. Real viewers don't behave like that. They

  • spend most of their time on salient content,
  • move between salient objects when there are several,
  • and every now and then leave them to explore the rest of the scene, then come back.

What they never do is get stuck, whether outside the salient content, frozen on one spot, or locked on to a single object. In this post we build a metric that rewards the behaviours above and penalizes getting stuck. We build it one case at a time, adding one piece to the equation at each step.

§1. Setup

  • The video has frames $t = 1, 2, \dots, \tau$. One step is one frame.
  • The model predicts a fixation $g(t) = \big(x(t), y(t)\big)$ at every frame.
  • $S_t$ is the ground-truth saliency map of frame $t$. It is made from the gaze of all human viewers, blurred with a Gaussian of width $\sigma$, and scaled so that its maximum is $1$.
  • The saliency at the prediction is $$w(t) = S_t\big(g(t)\big) \in [0, 1].$$ $w(t) \approx 1$ means the model looks where most people look. $w(t) \approx 0$ means it looks where nobody looks.
  • A fixation is inside a salient region if $w(t) \ge \theta$ and outside if $w(t) < \theta$. We use $\theta = 0.3$.
  • $\mathcal{C}$ is the set of frames where a new shot starts (scene cuts). The first frame counts as a cut, $1 \in \mathcal{C}$.

§2. The shape of every score

Every piece of the metric is built the same way:

$$ \text{score}(t) \;=\; \underbrace{\text{weight}(t) \times \text{amplitude}(t)}_{\text{how much a fixation is worth}} \;\times\; \underbrace{\text{decay}(t)}_{\text{how long it has kept doing this}} $$

The weight and the amplitude describe a single fixation. The decay has memory. It starts at $1$, shrinks every step the model keeps doing the same thing, and comes back up when the model does something new. All the cases below are about the decay.

The decay building block

We use one exponential decay throughout:

$$D(n) = p^{\,n/N}$$

  • $n$ is the number of steps the behaviour has lasted so far.
  • $N$ is a length scale in steps.
  • $p$ is the value the decay reaches after $N$ steps, because $D(N) = p$. We use $p = 0.01$.

This is an ordinary exponential, $p^{n/N} = e^{-n \ln(1/p)/N}$, with time constant $N/\ln(1/p)$. We write it with $p$ and $N$ because $N$ has a direct meaning that we can measure from human viewers: "after a typical human-length stretch of this behaviour, the score has dropped to $p$".

§3. Outside the salient region: exploration

3.1 How much a fixation is worth

Outside the salient region, a fixation counts as exploration. Two things decide how much it is worth.

  1. Weight $1 - w(t)$. The further the fixation is from where people look, the more it is exploration and the less it is a near miss of a salient object.
  2. Amplitude $A(t)$. Staring at a blank wall, the sky or a black letterbox bar is not exploring, so the amplitude measures how much there is to see around the fixation. $A(t)$ can be any score in $[0, 1]$ that captures semantic or structural information in the patch, for example its edge density. Here we use the patch entropy: we take the $b \times b$ box around $g(t)$ ($b = 50$ px), convert it to grey levels, put the pixels into $32$ intensity bins with fractions $q_1, \dots, q_{32}$, and set $$A(t) = H(t) = -\frac{1}{\log 32} \sum_{j=1}^{32} q_j \log q_j \;\in [0, 1].$$ A flat patch gives $H = 0$, and a richly textured patch gives $H$ close to $1$.

Before any decay, the exploration score is

$$E(t) = \big(1 - w(t)\big)\, A(t) \qquad \text{for } w(t) < \theta,$$

and $E(t) = 0$ inside the salient region.

3.2 Case 1: staying outside too long (global decay)

If the predicted fixation stays outside the salient regions for too long, the metric should penalize it gradually.

Try it: Figure 3.1 (below) runs this case on a toy video. Press Play to watch the score of each excursion fade, then drag $N_g$ or the number of frames outside to see how quickly a long excursion stops paying.

We count how long the model has been outside. The run counter $n(t)$ is the number of consecutive frames outside, including the current one:

$$ n(t) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } w(t) \ge \theta \quad \text{(inside: no run)}\\[2pt] 1 & \text{if } w(t) < \theta \text{ and } \big(w(t-1) \ge \theta \text{ or } t \in \mathcal{C}\big) \quad \text{(a run starts)}\\[2pt] n(t-1) + 1 & \text{otherwise} \quad \text{(the run continues)} \end{cases} $$

Every step outside multiplies the score by $p^{1/N_g}$:

$$E(t) = \big(1 - w(t)\big)\, A(t)\; p^{\,n(t)/N_g}.$$

  • Coming back resets it. As soon as the model returns to a salient region the run ends. Its next excursion starts again from $n = 1$. Short excursions are cheap, and long ones fade away.
  • A scene cut also resets it. A new shot is new content, so the counter starts over.
  • Choosing $N_g$. $N_g$ is the average length of a run outside the salient regions for human viewers. This is $6.29$ frames on DIEM and $3.07$ frames on Hollywood ($\sigma = 40$ px on screen, $\theta = 0.3$). So after an excursion as long as a typical human's, the score has fallen to $p = 1\%$.

3.3 Case 1.1: outside and not moving (local decay)

If the predicted fixation stays at the same position or very near it (it is basically not moving), it should be penalized immediately.

Try it: in Figure 3.2 (below), choose where the model freezes, or click single cells of the step strip to freeze them. The crimson segments are frozen (local) steps, and the dashed curve shows where the score would be if the model had kept moving.

The global decay treats "wandering around outside" and "frozen at one spot outside" the same. But the frozen model is not exploring at all, so every frozen step should cost more.

A local step. Put the $b \times b$ box $B(t)$ around $g(t)$. Frame $t$ is a local step if it continues a run and the box has barely moved since the previous frame:

$$\operatorname{IoU}\big(B(t), B(t-1)\big) > 0.9 .$$

For two equal squares shifted by $(\Delta x, \Delta y)$, the IoU has a closed form:

$$\operatorname{IoU} = \frac{(b - |\Delta x|)(b - |\Delta y|)}{2b^2 - (b - |\Delta x|)(b - |\Delta y|)} .$$

Show the proofHide the proof

Proof. Put $B(t-1)$ at $[0, b] \times [0, b]$, so that $B(t) = [\Delta x,\, \Delta x + b] \times [\Delta y,\, \Delta y + b]$. If $|\Delta x| \ge b$ or $|\Delta y| \ge b$, the boxes do not overlap and the IoU is $0$, so assume both shifts are smaller than $b$.

Intersection. Two axis-aligned rectangles meet in a rectangle, and each side of it is the overlap of the matching sides. Along $x$, $$[0, b] \cap [\Delta x,\, \Delta x + b] = \big[\max(0, \Delta x),\; \min(b,\, b + \Delta x)\big],$$ which has length $b - \Delta x$ if $\Delta x \ge 0$ and $b + \Delta x$ if $\Delta x < 0$, so $b - |\Delta x|$ in both cases. The same holds along $y$, so the intersection has area $$I = \big|B(t) \cap B(t-1)\big| = (b - |\Delta x|)(b - |\Delta y|).$$

Union. Adding the two areas counts the intersection twice, so we subtract it once (inclusion–exclusion): $$ \begin{aligned} \big|B(t) \cup B(t-1)\big| &= \big|B(t)\big| + \big|B(t-1)\big| - \big|B(t) \cap B(t-1)\big|\\ &= b^2 + b^2 - I \;=\; 2b^2 - I. \end{aligned} $$

Ratio. Dividing the two, $$\operatorname{IoU} = \frac{\big|B(t) \cap B(t-1)\big|}{\big|B(t) \cup B(t-1)\big|} = \frac{I}{2b^2 - I} = \frac{(b - |\Delta x|)(b - |\Delta y|)}{2b^2 - (b - |\Delta x|)(b - |\Delta y|)} .$$ ∎

For a shift of $d$ along one axis only ($|\Delta x| = d$, $\Delta y = 0$), the formula simplifies to

$$\operatorname{IoU} = \frac{b\,(b - d)}{2b^2 - b\,(b - d)} = \frac{b - d}{b + d},$$

and

$$\frac{b - d}{b + d} > 0.9 \iff b - d > 0.9\,b + 0.9\,d \iff 0.1\,b > 1.9\,d \iff d < \frac{b}{19}.$$

That is less than about $2.6$ px for $b = 50$. In other words, the model is not moving.

Every other step in the run, including the first one, is a global step. A second counter $\ell(t)$ counts the local steps in the current run:

$$ \ell(t) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if no run, or a run starts}\\ \ell(t-1) + 1 & \text{if } t \text{ is a local step}\\ \ell(t-1) & \text{otherwise,} \end{cases} $$

so the run's $n(t)$ steps split into $n(t) - \ell(t)$ global steps and $\ell(t)$ local steps. Each kind of step gets its own length scale, with $N_l < N_g$:

$$E(t) = \big(1 - w(t)\big)\, A(t)\; p^{\,(n(t) - \ell(t))/N_g \;+\; \ell(t)/N_l}.$$

$N_l$ is measured in the same way as $N_g$. It is the average length of human stretches outside where consecutive boxes overlap with IoU $> 0.9$: $2.42$ frames on DIEM and $2.22$ on Hollywood.

How much faster? On DIEM a global step multiplies the score by $0.01^{1/6.29} = 0.48$, while a local step multiplies it by $0.01^{1/2.42} = 0.15$. One frozen frame costs as much as about $2.6$ moving frames.

Freezing is not forgiven within the run. Once the model has frozen, the extra decay stays until the run ends, either when the model returns to a salient region or at a cut.

§4. Inside the salient region: tracking

The tracking term is built from the same pieces. It has its own counters and constants, and we reuse the letters $n$, $\ell$, $N_g$, $N_l$, $p$ for them. When both terms appear together we tag them with $E$ and $T$.

4.1 How much a fixation is worth

  • Weight $w(t)$. This is the mirror of the exploration weight $1 - w(t)$. The more salient the fixation, the more it counts as tracking.
  • Amplitude $A(t)$. The same as in exploration (§3.1): the patch entropy around the fixation.

$$T(t) = w(t)\, A(t) .$$

The tracking term needs no hard "inside" condition. Outside, $w(t) < \theta$ is small, so $T(t)$ is small automatically.

4.2 Case 2: staying only in salient regions (global decay)

If the predicted fixation only stays in the salient region, this should also be penalized globally, just like before.

Try it: in Figure 4.1 (below), change how long the model stays on the salient region before it leaves. Drag that slider to the end and the model never leaves, so its score just fades; otherwise the clock restarts every time it comes back.

Even perfect tracking should not go on forever without a look elsewhere. The tracking clock $n(t)$ counts frames since the model last came back to a salient region after being outside, or since the last cut:

$$ n(t) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } t \in \mathcal{C}, \text{ or } w(t) \ge \theta \text{ and } w(t-1) < \theta \quad \text{(cut, or came back)}\\[2pt] n(t-1) + 1 & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases} $$

$$T(t) = w(t)\, A(t)\; p^{\,n(t)/N_g} .$$

While the model is outside, the clock keeps running. It is not reset by leaving, only by returning. So the tracking reward is restored only after a real excursion.

Note the symmetry between the two terms:

  • the exploration counter is reset by entering a salient region,
  • the tracking clock is reset by coming back to a salient region after leaving.

Each term is reset by doing the other thing. A model gets high scores on both only if it keeps alternating between the two.

4.3 Case 2.2: several salient regions (local decay, reset on switch)

If there are several salient regions, the model needs to move between them, and should be penalized if it stays on only one. So a local decay is introduced. This local decay resets to the level of the global decay, which is the only decay when there is a single salient region.

Try it: in Figure 4.2 (below), change the number of salient regions and how long the model stays on each. With one region the curve is the plain global decay; with two or more, every switch lifts it back up to the dashed envelope.

Salient regions as peaks. The salient regions of frame $t$ are the peaks of $S_t$. A peak is a local maximum within radius $r = 2\sigma$ with height at least $\eta = 0.3$. Call the set of peaks $\mathcal{P}_t$. Each fixation is assigned to its nearest peak:

$$c(t) = \arg\min_{c \in \mathcal{P}_t} \big\lVert g(t) - c \big\rVert .$$

A switch. The model has moved to a different region if its assigned peak jumped by more than $r$:

$$\text{switch at } t \iff \big\lVert c(t) - c(t-1) \big\rVert > r .$$

Local steps since the last switch. If the frame has two or more peaks and the model is still on the same one, the frame is a local step:

$$ \ell(t) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if switch, cut, or came back}\\ \ell(t-1) + 1 & \text{if } |\mathcal{P}_t| \ge 2 \text{ and no switch}\\ \ell(t-1) & \text{if } |\mathcal{P}_t| = 1 . \end{cases} $$

As in exploration, the $n(t) - \ell(t)$ global steps use $N_g$ and the $\ell(t)$ local steps use the faster $N_l$:

$$T(t) = w(t)\, A(t)\; p^{\,(n(t) - \ell(t))/N_g \;+\; \ell(t)/N_l} .$$

Here is what this does:

  • Only one salient region. $\ell$ never grows, so this is exactly Case 2 (global decay only).
  • Several regions, staying on one. Each frame adds the fast $1/N_l$ decay.
  • A switch. $\ell$ drops to $0$ and the score jumps back up to the global envelope $$w(t)\, A(t)\; p^{\,n(t)/N_g},$$ not all the way back to $w(t)\, A(t)$. Switching between objects is good, but it is still "staying inside", so only an excursion outside restarts the global clock.

The result is a sawtooth under a slowly falling envelope.

§5. The final metric

Tagging the two terms' own counters and constants with $E$ and $T$, the two scores of the predicted fixation at frame $t$ are

$$ \begin{aligned} E(t) &= \mathbf{1}\big[w(t) < \theta\big]\,\big(1 - w(t)\big)\, A(t)\; p_E^{\,(n_E(t) - \ell_E(t))/N^E_g \,+\, \ell_E(t)/N^E_l} && \text{(exploration)}\\[6pt] T(t) &= w(t)\, A(t)\; p_T^{\,(n_T(t) - \ell_T(t))/N^T_g \,+\, \ell_T(t)/N^T_l} && \text{(tracking)} \end{aligned} $$

Over a video, we add up each term:

$$E_{\text{tot}} = \sum_{t=1}^{\tau} E(t), \qquad T_{\text{tot}} = \sum_{t=1}^{\tau} T(t).$$

A scanpath is good only when both totals are high, so the final metric is their geometric mean:

$$\boxed{\;M = \sqrt{E_{\text{tot}} \cdot T_{\text{tot}}}\;}$$

If either total is close to zero, so is $M$. A model that tracks well but never leaves the salient regions has $E_{\text{tot}} = 0$, and therefore $M = 0$.

Try it: Figure 5.1 (below) runs both terms on five example scanpaths. Pick one to see $E(t)$ and $T(t)$ frame by frame, then compare the totals: only the scanpath that both explores and tracks gets a high $M$.

Both terms have the same form, $\text{weight} \times \text{amplitude} \times p^{\,(\text{global steps})/N_g + (\text{local steps})/N_l}$. They differ only in three ways:

Exploration $E$ Tracking $T$
global step a frame outside a frame on salient content
local step box barely moved (IoU $> 0.9$) still on the same peak while $\ge 2$ peaks exist
local steps cleared by end of the run only switching to another peak
clock restarts on entering a salient region, cut coming back from outside, cut

Summary: what each behaviour does to the score

frozen at one spot outside faster local decay, $\times\, p_E^{1/N^E_l}$ per frame, never forgiven in the run
